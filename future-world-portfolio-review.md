## Key decisions and rationale

- EARLY is the deciding lens. Weight leans to the earliest, most convex themes
  (physical AI, quantum, space, gene editing) and trims the maturer ones (cyber,
  semis, medical robotics) without dropping them. Grid carries the top weight as
  the one near-certain bottleneck behind the entire AI buildout.
- Quantum: KEPT WisdomTree WQTM, not VanEck. WQTM is the purer, higher-beta
  pre-revenue basket (more collective weight in D-Wave, Rigetti, IonQ). Chosen for
  maximum early torque, consistent with the high-risk mandate. Caveat: its
  equal-weight rebuild harvests winners rather than letting them compound.
- Uranium: KEPT Sprott Junior Uranium Miners (URJP), not the broader fund.
  Juniors are the higher-beta, higher-return expression. Accepted trade-off: more
  dilution and single-name risk, and deeper mid-cycle drawdowns.
- Rare earths: NAMES not ETF. The clean ex-China ETF (REXC) is not on Trading 212,
  and the best available ETF is ~6% genuine rare earths with ~19% China. MP
  Materials and Lynas are the actual ex-China processing/magnet chokepoint, split
  3% each. The one place individual names beat the fund.
- Crypto: STRATEGY (MSTR) direct, not a spot Bitcoin ETP (which is not ISA-eligible
  from 6 April 2026) and not the diversified crypto ETF. MSTR is a leveraged
  Bitcoin proxy: it amplifies Bitcoin both ways, can trade at a premium or discount
  to the Bitcoin it holds, and carries dilution and financing risk. Sized at 5% as
  the deliberate high-torque moonshot and the one single-stock exception to the
  ETF-only rule.
- Semis (CHPX) added at 6%, kept modest. It is the upstream bottleneck (HBM,
  foundry, lithography, packaging) but the most mature, large-cap theme, so it is
  not oversized under the early lens.

## What was rejected and why

- VanEck Quantum, broad Sprott Uranium, spot Bitcoin, VanEck Crypto ETF: all
  overruled by Sam in favour of the higher-torque options above.
- DMAT to WisdomTree RARE swap: churn, RARE is barely better; went to names instead.
- WDNA to Xtrackers Genomic, STRR to VanEck Space: lateral, no edge, skipped.
- Copper, defence primes, datacentre-power: parked. Copper and primes are mature;
  AIPO (datacentre power) and DRON (drone autonomy) remain the only genuinely early
  additions if the pie is ever broadened.

## Implementation

- Wrapper: everything in the Stocks & Shares ISA. No CGT, no dividend tax, no
  reporting-fund admin. No spot crypto (not ISA-eligible); MSTR is a normal US
  equity and is fine in the ISA.
- Build it as a single Trading 212 pie with the target percentages above; the pie
  auto-allocates monthly contributions and supports fractional shares, so the 5-6%
  lines (~£365-438) and the stock lines size cleanly.
- Execution: limit orders, not market, on the thin lines (CHPX, STRR, WQTM, and
  the two rare-earth stocks). The liquid lines (WIRE, WCBR, FLXI, PAIG) can go at
  market. Roughly 0.15% FX applies on the USD lines (MP, Lynas, MSTR, CHPX etc).
- Rebalance: steer new monthly contributions into whichever line has drifted
  lightest (buy-only, free in the ISA). Only sell to correct a line that drifts
  beyond roughly plus or minus 5 points. Review quarterly, hard check each April.
- Behaviour: the whole design is hold-through-dips. The drawdowns are the
  volatility the mandate buys. Do not panic-sell; add on weakness.

## Caveats worth a glance before buying

- Several funds are weeks to months old (PAIG, WIRE, STRR, CHPX). Thin track
  record and wider spreads; closure risk is dismissed per the settled view, but
  use limit orders.
- MSTR is leveraged and premium-distorted, not 1:1 Bitcoin, and is a single stock
  held for a decade, the risk you deliberately designed out elsewhere. Eyes open.
- Rare earths is now two single stocks (MP, Lynas); concentrated by design.
- Confirm each line carries UK reporting-fund status on its factsheet before
  buying (standard for these Irish UCITS, but verify).
