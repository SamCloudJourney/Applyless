## Who Sam is

UK-based investor. Invests via Trading 212 (ISA and general account). British
spelling. Direct, institutional communication style. Dislikes em dashes and
hedged waffle. Wants decisive conclusions, not surveys of options. Owns the
bottleneck, not the narrative.

## What this portfolio is

One sleeve, not the whole net worth. The deliberately high-risk, high-conviction
"Future World" thematic sleeve. Horizon 10-30 years (roughly 2035-2045 and
beyond). Long-term capital Sam will not touch. Explicit goal: significant
capital gains, with high risk accepted as the price. Not a balanced portfolio.
Do not nudge toward caution Sam has not asked for.

## Selection philosophy

Governing principle across all of Sam's investing: own the bottleneck, not the
narrative. Hold the structural chokepoints of future industries rather than
chase hype. Other consistent filters: long holding horizon (3-10+ years, here
10-30), genuine structural demand over story stocks, hard valuation discipline
elsewhere in the book, concentrated complementary-team construction over
diversification for its own sake.

Rules for this sleeve:

- Every sleeve maps to a distinct future trend with no lazy overlap. Explicitly
  excluded: S&P 500, MSCI World, Nasdaq-100, generic AI ETFs. No filler.
- No duplication across wrappers, verified by look-through. Confirmed: the funds
  barely double-count individual shares.
- ETF-only by choice. Sam has deliberately stopped picking individual stocks for
  this long-horizon money. Over 10-30 years individual companies get acquired,
  disrupted or fade, while a thematic index quietly rotates into whoever wins
  (survivorship rotation). This is the single strongest argument for the wrapper
  over a horizon this long.

ETF vs concentrated basket: keep the ETF where the theme is genuinely
un-pickable (cyber, quantum, biotech, space, physical AI). A concentrated 3-5
name basket is only arguably better where the chokepoint is a short, dominant,
listed set (medical robotics, rare earths). Sam knows the youngest narrative
wrappers (quantum, tokenisation, space, humanoids) most contradict the "own the
bottleneck" philosophy, because they are baskets of narrative rather than
baskets of cash-generative chokepoints. Held as deliberate small bets anyway.

## Settled. Do not relitigate.

1. This is a satellite, not a standalone portfolio. No broad global equity core,
   no quality-compounder ballast, and ~48% sits in four correlated tech-cyclical
   themes (AI/cyber/quantum/grid) that move together in a drawdown. Sam knows
   this. Adding a boring global core underneath is a future structural decision,
   not a today problem.
2. Fund-closure risk is understood and dismissed as minor. Four wrappers are
   sub-scale or brand new (STRR ~£4m, TKNX ~£1.3m, CYBO and WDNA ~£9-11m each).
   If a fund closes, Sam does not lose the capital; the holdings are sold and
   cash returned at NAV. Worst case is admin hassle and a possible taxable event
   outside an ISA. Comfortable with this. Top-tier issuers (BlackRock,
   WisdomTree, Global X), ISA-wrapped. Do not re-raise this as a scary risk.
3. New funds are a feature, not a flaw, for this horizon. In near inception means
   early to the theme, not buying after inflows have chased valuations up.
   Reputable issuers with distribution muscle. Accepted.
4. Blended cost ~0.46%/yr. Reasonable for thematic. Settled.
5. The largest hidden single-stock bets are not megacaps. Because URJP and FLXI
   are internally concentrated, the biggest look-through positions are a uranium
   junior (Paladin, ~1.15%) and Indian large caps, not NVIDIA (~0.5%).
